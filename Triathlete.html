Lightning-Fast Ironman Kalmar Wins for Laura Philipp and Jonas Schomburg Preview Exceptional Kona Form

In a record-breaking, spectator-filled race, Laura Philipp and Jonas Schomburg put on a show to remember, producing two of the fastest Ironman performances in history and showcasing world championship-winning form.

It was swim-bike-run perfection for Jonas Schomburg (GER), with a start-to-finish first Ironman win that showed almost no weakness. A sub-45-minute swim, four-hour bike, and sub-2:35 marathon saw him at the front all day and will surely have put his Kona rivals on notice with a 7:22:57.

Many questioned Laura Philipp’s (GER) decision to race an Ironman so close to Kona, with historical data suggesting it rarely leads to a great race on the Big Island. However, she decided to test her strong run of training form, and it paid off with a clear victory following one of her best swim-bike performances to date, eventually winning by close to 20 minutes.

On a windy day in Kalmar, Sweden, arguably the biggest storylines centered around who would pick up one of the final three Kona slots of the year and the last full-distance Pro Series points on offer before the championship season truly begins. Also, with many set to race Kona – how would their form be less than two months before the cannon goes off?

Kalmar is known for historically fast swim splits, with a uniquely shaped swim course lined with spectators. On the men’s side, there was talk of another “WhatsApp group” circulating to discuss swim tactics and push the pace hard enough to leave key rivals behind.

Clearly, it paid off, with a big breakaway at the front that included Schomburg but left Matthew Marquardt (USA) and Daniel Baekkegard (DEN) over a minute behind, while also putting Trevor Foley (USA) into an early deficit of nearly four minutes. However, for him, that was one of, if not the best, Ironman swims of his career – well within striking distance of the front.

On the women’s side, things were much more straightforward, with strong swimmer Rachel Zilinskas (USA) hitting the front early and opening a significant gap over the women’s field to begin the bike alone three minutes ahead.

Behind her, however, pre-race favorite Philipp had a great swim and said afterward that she was “feeling good” in the water, even leading parts of her large swim pack – something she doesn’t usually do.

Schomburg unsurprisingly played his usual tactic of starting quickly and taking the lead out of transition, but from there he changed how he played his cards compared to previous races and appeared to hold back slightly on the bike.

That allowed riders like Marquardt and Baekkegard to get back to him and form a group. Clearly, Schomburg was mixing things up from his performance in Roth and saving something in the tank for the run.

In doing so, home hopeful Robert Kallin (SWE) stole the spotlight on the bike, riding 3:53:12 – a new world-best Ironman bike split – to gap everyone behind and start the run with the home crowd behind him and a four-minute advantage over the chasing group.

Onto the marathon, and the chase for Kona slots became almost as interesting as the race for the win. Veteran Andreas Salvisberg (SUI) started the run in contention behind the main group, donning a hydration vest like an ultrarunner,  and was one of four athletes at the front battling for just three remaining spots.

Schomburg, in his trademark fashion, shot out of transition and immediately left Marquardt and the rest of the group behind. He eventually overtook Kallin around the 10km mark and already earned roughly a three-minute gap to Marquardt, who had slipped out of the podium positions behind 2024 Pro Series winner Gregory Barnaby (ITA).

Further back, Foley started the run having clawed his way up to 11th, but at around 12 minutes back, even a strong opening to the marathon left him with too much ground to make up.

The rest of the run developed dramatically even as Schomburg continued to distance himself. Marquardt remained off the podium until the final 8km, when Barnaby began faded dramatically from second toward fourth. Salvisberg then also began to struggle to maintain his early pace, while Marquardt paced his marathon to perfection and eventually earned second, five and a half minutes down.

Barnaby and Salvisberg, meanwhile, hung on out of sheer determination (and likely some desperation) to secure their last-chance Kona slots,

Schomburg’s win, however, deserves all the spotlight and puts the Kona field firmly on notice. This was one of the fastest Ironman performances in history and showed that when he gets everything right all the way to the finish, he has very few weaknesses and the speed to rival that of any athlete in the world. Furthermore, as an athlete who thrives in the heat, his potential in Kona may be even greater.

Women’s Race

Out of T2 in second place with only around three minutes to make up on Zilinskas, Philipp found herself in an almost perfect scenario while racing the new Canyon Speedmax for the first time. She had someone to chase who was well within her grasp and could envision clear daylight ahead.

By 50km, she was already into the lead by more than a minute and in pole position.

Behind her was perhaps her biggest threat, Katrine Græsbøll Christensen (DEN), who had outbiked Philipp by more than five minutes not long before in Hamburg. Christensen was again riding faster, but after a slower swim still remained several minutes in arrears.

By the end of the bike, Philipp had not only held her off but paced the ride more consistently, starting the marathon with a gap that grew back out to roughly five and a half minutes.

Further behind was Jackie Hering (USA), racing with relatively few miles in her legs following an injury. She entered T2 around 10 minutes back in fifth but remained firmly involved and would begin a nail-biting but grueling battle for the podium.

The run unfolded largely as expected for an energetic Philipp, who seemed to be smiling all day and soaking up the crowd. Although she struggled slightly toward the end of the marathon, she still comfortably outran the field with a 2:52 marathon split to win emphatically, describing the race afterward as “one of a kind.”

Like the men’s race, however, the battle behind for the podium places was riveting.

Zilinskas began to fall off the pace while Hering moved into fourth behind Jenny Jendryschik (GER), who was putting together a career-best performance.

Hering eventually overtook Jendryschik after 25km and appeared on pace to catch Christensen before the finish. But the closing stages of the marathon became almost a race of survival for all three women, with temperatures rising and each looking close to the edge of their limits.

In the end, Christensen collapsed across the finish line to secure second ahead of Hering, who was similarly exhausted as she crossed in third – an especially impressive result considering the timeline of her return from injury.

The gap to Philipp ahead, like Schomburg, will have her closest Kona competitors rethinking her potential for Kona, which looked in doubt before the race amid a topsy-turvy 2026 season. Now she looks in prime position to fight for another championship title.
